Statutory Classification

What is Trademark Class 5 in India?

Under the International NICE Classification system administered by WIPO and enforced by the Trade Marks Registry (Plot No. 32, Sector 14, Dwarka, New Delhi 110078), Class 5 is the exclusive statutory class covering pharmaceuticals, medical preparations, veterinary drugs, and therapeutic health substances.

Allopathic Medicines

Prescription drugs (Schedule H, H1, X), over-the-counter (OTC) medications, injectables, syrups, tablets, antibiotics, and dermatological ointments manufactured or marketed across Delhi NCR.

Ayurvedic & AYUSH Brands

Proprietary Ayurvedic medicines, herbal supplements, Unani and Siddha preparations, and homeopathic remedies requiring distinct proprietary coined naming to avoid public domain rejection.

Nutraceuticals & Disinfectants

Dietary supplements adapted for medical use, vitamins, baby food, hospital-grade disinfectants, medical dressings, sanitary preparations, and surgical plasters.

Supreme Court Landmark Doctrine

The Cadila Principle: Why Pharma Trademarks Face Stricter Scrutiny

In the landmark judgment Cadila Health Care Ltd. v. Cadila Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (2001) 5 SCC 73, the Supreme Court of India ruled that a much stricter test of deceptive similarity must be applied to medicinal preparations compared to ordinary commercial goods.

The rationale is paramount public safety: a consumer mistaking one garment or software brand for another incurs financial inconvenience; mistaking one medicine for another can lead to severe adverse reactions or loss of life.

Key Cadila Rules Applied by Dwarka Registry:

  • Schedule H Prescription is Not a Total Shield: Even prescription drugs are often dispensed over telephone, through poorly handwritten doctor prescriptions, or in noisy chemist shops.
  • Phonetic Similarity Overweighs Visual Nuance: Names that sound alike when spoken aloud in Hindi or English (e.g., 'Falcigo' vs 'Falcitab') create unacceptable confusion.
  • Class of Consumers: Rural and semi-literate patients may not distinguish subtle letter changes on packaging.

Section 9 & 11 Objections in Class 5

1. Section 13 Bar: INN Names & Chemical Elements

Trade Marks Act 1999 Section 13 explicitly prohibits the registration of words declared by the WHO as International Nonproprietary Names (INN) or chemical salts (e.g., Paracetamol, Cetirizine, Azithromycin). Any trademark derived closely from generic chemical stems is summarily refused.

2. Section 9(1)(b): Descriptive Disease or Organ Names

Names like 'Gastro-Cure', 'Cardio-Fit', or 'Dermo-Clean' are routinely refused for directly describing the targeted organ, disease, or therapeutic efficacy without secondary meaning.

3. Section 11(1): Prior Registered Conflicting Marks

With over 300,000 live marks in Class 5 on the Indian Register, common prefixes (like 'Cef-', 'Pan-', 'Cal-', 'Neuro-') lead to automated examination objection reports requiring rigorous advocate argument.
