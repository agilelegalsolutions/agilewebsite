What is intellectual property law

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) are legal rights that protect creations of the human mind under Indian law. The four primary statutory rights comprise trademarks (protecting brand names, logos, and commercial slogans), copyrights (protecting original literary, artistic, and software works), patents (protecting novel and industrially applicable functional inventions), and industrial designs (protecting aesthetic visual product shapes and configurations).

Statutory framework of intellectual property rights in India

Each intellectual property right is governed by dedicated legislation, distinct administrative authorities, and specific protection durations. The table below summarizes the statutory framework applicable to applicants in Delhi:

Right What It Protects Filing Authority Duration of Protection Governing Statute
Trademark Brand names, logos, taglines, composite device marks, sound marks Trade Marks Registry (Dwarka, New Delhi) 10 years (renewable every 10 years indefinitely) Trade Marks Act, 1999
Copyright Literary works, software source code, artistic graphics, music, cinematograph films Copyright Office (Boudhik Sampada Bhawan, Dwarka) Life of the author + 60 years Copyright Act, 1957
Patent Novel technical inventions, industrial manufacturing processes, machines, compositions Patent Office (Boudhik Sampada Bhawan, Dwarka) 20 years from the international/domestic filing date Patents Act, 1970
Industrial Design Novel aesthetic shapes, geometric configurations, patterns, and ornaments applied to articles Patent & Design Office (CGPDTM) 10 years (extendable by 5 years to 15 years total) Designs Act, 2000

Note: Verify against current statutes. Statutory durations and renewal requirements are subject to procedural compliance under applicable rules.

When to involve an IP lawyer

Engaging an intellectual property attorney early mitigates substantial financial and legal exposure. Key situations requiring legal review include:

1. Before launching a brand

Prior to committing capital to marketing collateral, signage, or domains, legal clearance verifies whether your brand name conflicts with active trademarks.

2. Before marketplace listing

Major e-commerce platforms require verified trademark filing receipts or registration numbers to unlock brand protection and prevent unauthorized mapping.

3. When a copycat is discovered

Prompt issuance of a structured cease-and-desist notice establishes priority of use and creates formal documentation required for injunction proceedings.

4. When an objection or opposition arises

Statutory deadlines to reply to examination reports (30 days) and counter-statements (2 months) are non-extendable; missing them causes application abandonment.

5. When hiring external creators

Independent contractors retain copyright in software code, logos, and copy unless explicit written copyright assignment covenants are executed under Section 19.

6. During investor due diligence

Venture capital and private equity investors scrutinize intellectual property ownership chains to ensure all commercial assets are unencumbered.
