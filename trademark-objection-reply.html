Receiving an Examination Report with objections from the Trade Marks Registry does not mean your trademark has been rejected. It is a standard procedural stage under Section 9 and Section 11 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 where the Examiner seeks statutory clarification on the distinctiveness or conflict profile of your brand name.

Strict 30-Day Mandatory Deadline

Under Rule 33 of Trade Marks Rules, 2017, you must submit a written legal reply within 30 days from the date of dispatch of the Examination Report. Failure to submit a timely response will cause the Registrar to deem the application "Abandoned".

Understanding the Main Types of Trademark Objections

Over 95% of examination objections fall into two distinct legal categories under the Act:

1. Section 9(1): Absolute Grounds of Refusal

Section 9 objections arise when the Examiner believes the mark:

  • Lacks Distinctiveness: The word is too generic or common in everyday language.
  • Is Descriptive: Directly indicates the quality, quantity, intended purpose, value, or geographical origin of the goods/services (e.g., trying to trademark "Best Cold Coffee").
  • Has Become Customary: Is widely used in the established trade practices of that industry.
Our Defense Strategy: Proving acquired distinctiveness through long prior use (Section 9 proviso), presenting user affidavits, sales invoices, brand marketing expenditures, and demonstrating the mark has developed a secondary meaning.

2. Section 11(1): Relative Grounds of Refusal

Section 11 objections arise when the Registry cites earlier conflicting marks on record:

  • Identical or Deceptively Similar: Phonetic, visual, or conceptual similarity with an earlier registered or pending trademark.
  • Similarity of Goods/Services: Operating in identical, overlapping, or commercially related product categories creating public confusion.
Our Defense Strategy: Distinguishing phonetic and visual structures, demonstrating difference in target consumer bases, trade channels, price points, and citing landmark Supreme Court and High Court IP precedents (e.g., Cadila Healthcare, Anti-Dissection Rule).

A poorly drafted, generic reply often results in immediate refusal. Our registered Trademark Advocates execute a rigorous legal protocol:

1
Deep Examination Report Analysis

We inspect the cited conflicting marks, checking their status (registered, opposed, abandoned, or expired) and evaluating prior user dates.

Statutory Counter-Statement Drafting

Drafting point-by-point rebuttal grounded in statutory provisions of Trade Marks Act 1999 and authoritative High Court case laws.

3

If the Examiner marks the application for a "Show Cause Hearing", our senior advocates appear before the Hearing Officer at the Trade Marks Registry (Dwarka, Delhi or via Video Conferencing) to present oral arguments.
