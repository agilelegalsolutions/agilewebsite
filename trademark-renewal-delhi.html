Statutory Mandate

How Trademark Renewal Works Under Indian Law

Under Section 25 of the Trade Marks Act 1999 and Rules 57-61 of Trade Marks Rules 2017, a registered trademark grants exclusive statutory ownership for an initial term of 10 years. To keep the registration active, timely renewal via Form TM-R is mandatory.

Window 1: Timely Renewal

Within 6 Months Prior to Expiration: The safest, most cost-effective window. File Form TM-R online with the standard statutory fee of Rs 9,000 per class. Avoids penalties, risk of abandonment, or third-party challenges.

Window 2: Grace Period with Surcharge

Within 6 Months After Expiration: Section 25(3) provides a 6-month grace period. Form TM-R must be accompanied by the statutory late surcharge of Rs 4,500 (total official fee of Rs 13,500 per class).

Window 3: Restoration & Renewal

Within 1 Year from Expiration: If removed from the Register for non-renewal, the proprietor can file for Restoration & Renewal under Section 25(4) and Rule 60. Official fee is Rs 18,000 per class with statement of grounds.

Procedural Safeguard

The Mandatory O-3 Notice: Safeguarding Your Brand Against Unlawful Removal

Section 25(3) of the Trade Marks Act 1999 places an explicit statutory obligation upon the Registrar of Trade Marks: before removing any registered trademark from the Register for non-payment of renewal fees, the Registrar must issue an official notice in Form O-3 to the registered proprietor at their registered address for service.

In multiple landmark decisions, the Delhi High Court has reaffirmed that administrative removal of a trademark without strictly complying with the mandatory service of Form O-3 notice is void ab initio and bad in law.

Delhi High Court Restoration Precedents:

Where an applicant never received the physical or electronic O-3 notice, Advocate Arun Kumar Jha files specialized representation before the Trade Marks Registry Dwarka, establishing failure of statutory service to successfully restore the brand to the Register even when standard administrative timelines have lapsed.

Severe Risks of Letting Your Trademark Lapse

1. Loss of Statutory Exclusive Rights (Section 28)

Once removed, you can no longer initiate trademark infringement suits under Section 29; your legal remedy is weakened and relegated to costly, time-consuming common law passing-off litigation.

2. Squatters & Competitor Takeovers

Competitors regularly track trademark renewal deadlines. If your mark is abandoned, opportunistic third parties can file a fresh application for your identical brand name and seize your established goodwill.

3. Invalidation of E-Commerce & Customs Registrations

Amazon Brand Registry, Flipkart Brand Protection, and Indian Customs IPR enforcement portals automatically suspend brand protection privileges when trademark validity records show 'Removed' or 'Abandoned'.

Frequently Asked Questions

Trademark Renewal & Restoration FAQs

Clear legal guidance on Form TM-R deadlines, statutory costs, and restoration under Trade Marks Act 1999.

Under Section 25 of the Trade Marks Act 1999, a trademark registration is valid for 10 years from the date of application. An application for renewal (Form TM-R) can be filed up to 6 months before the expiration date to maintain seamless continuous legal protection.

The statutory government fee for online standard renewal under Form TM-R is Rs 9,000 per class per mark. Unlike initial registration under Form TM-A, the Trade Marks Rules do not offer MSME or startup fee concessions for trademark renewals.

If not renewed before expiry, the mark enters a 6-month statutory grace period under Section 25(3). It can still be renewed during this window by paying the Rs 9,000 renewal fee plus a Rs 4,500 late surcharge (total Rs 13,500 per class).

Yes. If not renewed within the 6-month grace period and removed from the Register, the proprietor can file for Restoration and Renewal under Section 25(4) and Rule 60 within 1 year from expiration. The official fee is Rs 18,000 per class (Rs 9,000 restoration plus Rs 9,000 renewal).

Under Section 25(3) of the Act and Rule 58, the Registrar is legally obligated to send an official notice in Form O-3 to the registered proprietor at their address for service before removing any mark. Delhi High Court judgments hold that removal without serving an O-3 notice is unlawful.

Form TM-R is strictly for renewing the registered mark as it stands. To update your corporate address or trade name, file Form TM-P. Substantial changes to the trademark logo or word itself are not permitted on renewal; a new application (Form TM-A) must be filed.
