What is a trademark opposition?

A trademark opposition is a formal adversarial proceeding under Section 21 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 where a third party challenges the registration of an advertised mark. Unlike examination objections raised internally by the Registry, an opposition is contested litigation between two commercial parties before the Trade Marks Registry.

1. Critical Statutory Timelines & Form TM-O

Opposition proceedings follow strict, non-extendable statutory milestones governed by the Trade Marks Rules, 2017:

Stage Procedural Requirement Statutory Deadline
Notice of Opposition Opponent files Form TM-O specifying legal grounds under Section 9, 11, or 18. Within 4 months of Journal publication
Counter-Statement Applicant files Form TM-O rebutting every factual and legal allegation. Strict 2 Months from receipt (Non-extendable)
Rule 45 Evidence Opponent submits evidence affidavit supporting opposition grounds. Within 2 months of receiving counter-statement
Rule 46 Evidence Applicant submits evidence affidavit proving prior use, turnover, and market reputation. Within 2 months of receiving Rule 45 evidence
Rule 47 Evidence in Reply Opponent submits confined rebuttal evidence strictly confined to matters in Rule 46. Within 1 month of receiving Rule 46 evidence
Final Opposition Hearing Oral arguments presented by Advocates before the Hearing Officer at Dwarka Registry. Date scheduled by Registry

2. Common Grounds for Trademark Opposition in Delhi

Oppositions in Delhi typically invoke specific statutory provisions:

Section 11: Prior Conflicting Rights

The most frequent ground: alleging that the applied-for mark is deceptively similar or phonetically identical to an earlier registered or pending trademark, creating consumer confusion in common trade channels.

Section 18 & Bad Faith Adoption

Alleging that the applicant is not the true proprietor of the mark, adopted the brand in bad faith, or copied a well-known international or domestic brand to pass off counterfeit goods.

3. Evidentiary Strategy Under Rules 45 & 46

Unlike initial filings, opposition proceedings are won or lost on evidentiary documentation. Our advocates, practicing in Delhi High Court and Registry matters since 2017, construct robust evidentiary records comprising:

  1. Affidavits of Evidence: Sworn affidavits detailing the commercial inception, continuous territorial use, and brand history.
  2. Turnover & CA Certificates: Chartered Accountant certified annual sales figures demonstrating commercial presence across Delhi NCR and India.
  3. Commercial Invoices: Continuous, date-stamped GST invoices establishing prior user dates under Rule 25.
  4. Promotional Evidence: Print advertisements, digital marketing expenditures, exhibition participations, and social media reach establishing secondary meaning.

4. Objection vs. Opposition: Key Differences

Applicants often confuse Registry objections with third-party oppositions:

  • Trademark Objection: Raised by the government examiner under Section 9 or 11. Handled via a written legal response within 30 days. Learn more in our trademark objection reply guide.
  • Trademark Opposition: Initiated by an external business competitor after Journal publication. Requires formal adversarial pleadings (Form TM-O), evidence rounds, and final oral advocacy before a Hearing Officer. Learn more about hearing procedures in our trademark hearing guide.

Frequently Asked Questions: Trademark Opposition in Delhi

A trademark opposition is initiated under Section 21 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 when a third party files a formal Notice of Opposition on Form TM-O within four months of the trademark being advertised in the official Trade Marks Journal.

The applicant must file a formal Counter-Statement on Form TM-O within exactly two months from the date of receiving the notice of opposition from the Trade Marks Registry. This two-month statutory timeline is non-extendable under Indian law.

If the applicant fails to submit a counter-statement within the strict two-month statutory window, the Trade Marks Registry treats the trademark application as abandoned for lack of prosecution under Section 21(2).

The opponent first files evidence in support of opposition under Rule 45. Subsequently, the applicant files evidence in support of the application under Rule 46 via an affidavit accompanied by commercial invoices, audited balance sheets, advertising expenditures, and prior user proofs.

Opposition hearings for applicants in the northern region are adjudicated by Hearing Officers at the Trade Marks Registry located at Boudhik Sampada Bhawan, Plot No. 32, Sector 14, Dwarka, New Delhi 110078, typically conducted via video conference.
