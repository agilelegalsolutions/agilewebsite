Information & Guidance

Frequently Asked Questions

Information regarding trademark procedures and legal requirements in India.

Why hire a specialized Intellectual Property Lawyer in Delhi?

An experienced Intellectual Property Lawyer in Delhi specializes in statutory rights under the Trade Marks Act 1999, Patents Act, and Copyright Act. Advocate Arun Kumar Jha provides advocacy before the Delhi High Court and Trade Marks Registry (Dwarka), ensuring proper class protection, robust objection replies under Sections 9 and 11, and defending commercial trademarks.

What is a trademark, and why is registration necessary?

A trademark is a distinctive visual sign, brand name, logo, or tagline that distinguishes your goods and services. Registering it grants exclusive statutory rights under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 across India.

Can I use the ™ symbol immediately after filing?

Yes. As soon as your TM-A application is submitted online and the official government filing receipt with your unique application number is generated, you are legally entitled to display the ™ symbol beside your brand name and logo.

How long does trademark registration take in India?

Upon filing Form TM-A, the application number is issued. The full examination, advertisement in the official Trade Marks Journal, and final issuance of the 10-year official ® Registration Certificate generally takes 6 to 12 months if uncontested.

What are the government fees for trademark registration?

The official statutory Government fees under the Trade Marks Rules, 2017 are:
  • ₹4,500 per class: For Individuals, Sole Proprietors, DPIIT-recognized Startups, and MSME/Udyam registered entities (50% fee concession).
  • ₹9,000 per class: For Companies, LLPs, and other entities without MSME certificates.
Confirm current fees on the IP India website.

What documents are required to file a trademark application?

Standard requirements include: (1) Brand name or logo image file, (2) Applicant Identity & Address proof (PAN card / Aadhaar / GST certificate), (3) MSME/Udyam Certificate if claiming the 50% statutory fee concession, and (4) Form TM-M (Power of Attorney) authorizing our advocate to file on your behalf.

What happens if my trademark application receives an objection?

If the Trade Marks Registry raises an examination report objection under Section 9 (lack of distinctiveness) or Section 11 (similar existing marks), our High Court advocates draft and file a formal legal reply along with evidence of prior commercial use and relevant case laws.
