What triggers a trademark hearing in Delhi?

A trademark show-cause hearing is scheduled under Section 18(4) of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 when the Registrar considers an applicant's written reply to an examination report insufficient to waive statutory objections. For northern India, hearings are conducted by the Trade Marks Registry at Boudhik Sampada Bhawan in Dwarka, New Delhi.

1. Understanding the Show Cause Hearing Notice

When an examination report reply does not satisfy the examiner, the application status changes on the IP India portal to "Ready for Show Cause Hearing" or "Hearing Notice Issued". The Trade Marks Registry issues a formal hearing notice specifying the date, time, and hearing officer assigned.

Hearings in the Delhi Registry are primarily conducted through video conferencing (Cisco Webex). If the applicant or their authorized advocate fails to appear on the scheduled date, the application risks being marked as "Abandoned" for lack of prosecution under the Trade Marks Rules, 2017.

2. Common Grounds Argued in Delhi Trademark Hearings

Hearings typically revolve around two statutory pillars of the Trade Marks Act, 1999:

Section 9: Absolute Grounds

Arguments center on demonstrating that the trademark has acquired distinctiveness through prior continuous commercial use. Counsel presents user affidavits, turnover figures, tax invoices, and promotional records under Rule 25.

Section 11: Relative Grounds

Arguments focus on disproving likelihood of confusion with cited marks. Counsel establishes visual, phonetic, and conceptual differences, market channel segmentation, or presents limitation of goods.

3. Step-by-Step Procedure for Delhi Registry Hearings

  1. Cause List Verification: The cause list is published on the official IP India website with the serial numbers and virtual meeting link.
  2. Filing Written Submissions (Form TM-M): Prior to the hearing date, our advocates prepare comprehensive written arguments with supporting judicial precedents and upload them via the e-filing portal.
  3. Virtual Attendance: The advocate joins the designated virtual bench before the Assistant Registrar or Hearing Officer at Boudhik Sampada Bhawan.
  4. Oral Submissions: Legal counsel presents arguments addressing each specific objection, referencing Delhi High Court judgments and statutory exemptions.
  5. Order Passed: The hearing officer either accepts the mark, orders advertisement in the TM Journal, imposes conditions, or reserves orders.

4. Trade Marks Registry Jurisdiction (Dwarka, New Delhi)

The Trade Marks Registry located in Sector 14, Dwarka exercises territorial jurisdiction over applications originating from:

  • National Capital Territory of Delhi
  • State of Haryana (Gurugram, Faridabad, Panipat, Sonipat)
  • State of Punjab (Ludhiana, Amritsar, Jalandhar)
  • State of Himachal Pradesh & Uttarakhand
  • Union Territories of Chandigarh, Jammu & Kashmir, and Ladakh
